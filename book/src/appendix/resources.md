# Appendix F - Open Resources for Packet Tracer Practice

Extra practice beyond this book's own labs. Everything below is free and does not require anything paid; the community links were checked for recent activity before being added here, but web resources do move - if a link is dead, search the title directly.

---

## Official (Cisco / Networking Academy)

- **[Cisco Packet Tracer - NetAcad](https://www.netacad.com/cisco-packet-tracer)** - the official Packet Tracer page: what it is, release notes, and the download path.
- **["Introduction to Packet Tracer" - Skills for All](https://skillsforall.com)** - the free course this book's Module 1 Pre-Lab already has you enroll in for the installer. Beyond the install, it has its own short guided activities covering the same panels this book's Module 1 introduces - useful as a second pass if the UI still feels unfamiliar after Module 1.

## Community lab collections

- **[kimmknight/awesome-packet-tracer](https://github.com/kimmknight/awesome-packet-tracer)** - a curated list of Packet Tracer tools, extensions, and resources maintained by the community. Good starting point if you want to go looking for more on your own rather than follow a specific link here.
- **[O2sa/CCNA-Labs](https://github.com/O2sa/CCNA-Labs)** - a lab collection covering OSPF, EIGRP, VLANs, ACLs, and WAN topics - directly overlapping this course's Modules 6, 7, 9, 10, and 11. Useful for extra reps on the same protocols with different topologies than this book uses.
- **[PPechmann/Packet-Tracer-Practice-Labs](https://github.com/PPechmann/Packet-Tracer-Practice-Labs)** - beginner-to-CCNA-level practice labs. Good for extra reps on the fundamentals from Modules 1-4 before you move into routing.

## How to use these alongside this book

This book's own labs are the required material - they are what your lab reports and the midterm/final are graded against (see [Appendix A](command-reference.md) for the exact commands used throughout). The resources above are for students who finish a Guided Lab early, want a topology that isn't the one this book walks you through, or want to see the same protocol configured a different way before an exam. None of them replace the Pre-Lab reading or the Deliverables checklist in each module.
